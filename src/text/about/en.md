Photography is about observing, but here it's about listening. It's not about the quiet whisper of a cowboy in the USA or hearing the rhythmic dancing of a boxer in a gym in Havana.

Henk Bleeker (Hallum, 1969) is a prominent Dutch art and documentary photographer from Friesland who graduated from the Royal Academy of Art in The Hague in 1994. His work lies at the intersection of documentary photography, portraiture, and autonomous art photography. He is known for his atmospheric, cinematic images, in which he puts human resilience, authenticity and life outside conventional society at the centre. He has worked on both commissioned projects, such as those for fashion, theatre and culture, and free projects.

Bleeker's photography is characterised by:

Authenticity over perfection. He seeks out people, places and situations with a distinctive character. His subjects are often cowboys, boxers, actors, craftsmen, models and people from isolated or traditional communities.

A cinematographic appearance. His photos are carefully constructed, making expressive use of light and shadow. They often look dramatic and timeless.

Storytelling: He doesn't just want to record what he sees; he also wants to convey the atmosphere and emotional impact of a subject. His images are therefore more interpretations than mere registrations.

This is Henk's working method. Bleeker's approach is naturally highly personal. It starts with observation and establishing contact with the subject, and then, as I call it, the improvisation begins. When I go out to take photos for a story, I think about how it will go. What will I find? Will this work? All the doubts and uncertainty, all the choices – any serious photographer will be familiar with these.

I work both on location and in my well-equipped studio on the outskirts of Friesland, where I often create powerful commissioned portraits. When working on location, I look for environments that contribute to the story. I grew up with film in the analogue era of photography. These days, I work digitally, striving to give the final print the look and feel of a darkroom print.

## International Projects and 'Menonitas'

Although deeply rooted in Friesland, his work took him all over the world, with photo stories in Cuba, Japan, the United States and South America.

One of his most talked-about and recent projects is his documentation of the Mennonites in Paraguay. In 2025, Bleeker was given the unique opportunity to gain access to this extremely closed, traditional religious community. His own Frisian background opened doors, as the history of the Mennonites is closely linked to the 16th-century Frisian reformer Menno Simons.

With this series, titled Menonitas | A World Outside Our Time, he captured the simplicity, the identity and the deep traditions of the community. The moving portraits and sweeping landscapes (such as the well-known work “Sisterhood”) resulted in a highly acclaimed exhibition at, among others, Museum Martena in Franeker, and exhibitions in Paraguay itself at BGN Arte in Asuncion.
