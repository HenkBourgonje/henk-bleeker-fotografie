---
title: About Henk
---

It was a Tuesday evening in 1989, somewhere in the past.

In the dojo of the legendary karate trainer Raymond Snel in Heerenveen, we trained with everything we had.

To me, our village back then was a sleepy place. But the sport gave us a purpose, and it felt great to do. We had a lot of respect for each other and were always looking to push our limits.

On one of those evenings I heard a voice call out: “Hey Henk, you're into photography, right?” It was my training buddy, the music journalist Harry de Jong. “You bet!” I shouted through the warm, steaming shower water. “In September I'm going to The Hague to study photography at the KABK.” “Sounds good,” he said. “Let's go out on assignment together soon.” And so we did, what an adventure!

Before long I got the chance to put big international stars in front of my lens, from ZZ Top to B.B. King and Golden Earring, among many others.

It was a dream come true. But after a while I discovered my own path in photography.

Less rock-and-roll and more fine art photography, with the emphasis on aesthetics.

Ton Kinsbergen became my new inspiration, he was a perfect fit for me. Technically he was exceptional, and a true master at playing with light.

The Sinar 4x5 inch camera or bigger became my standard gear, and I created images in an enormous studio that felt like a universe of its own.

Now, thirty years later, photography has brought me the world. And I am deeply grateful for everything that has come my way.

My son once asked me: “Heit, what's your best photo?” My answer was: “I still have to take that one.”
